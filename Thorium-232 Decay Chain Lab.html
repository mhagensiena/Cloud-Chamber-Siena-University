Build your model

  1. Turn on Predict first. For Th-232, which kind of decay do you expect, and why?
  2. Run the whole chain. In the ledger, find what happens to protons, neutrons, and mass number in an alpha decay and in a beta decay.
  3. Sketch a before and after nucleus for one alpha decay and one beta decay. Label the particle that leaves.
  4. A gamma ray follows some decays. What changes in the nucleus when it leaves, and what does not?
  5. Bismuth-212 can decay two ways. Reset and run the chain again to find the other path. Where do both paths end?
  6. Compare the energy bar for a decay with a chemical reaction. What does that say about the forces inside a nucleus?
Notes for teachers
  • Standard. HS-PS1-8 asks students to develop models of how the nucleus changes, and the energy released, in fission, fusion, and radioactive decay. This lab covers radioactive decay only (alpha, beta, and gamma). Pair it with a separate model for fission and fusion.
  • Practice. Developing and using models. Students predict, observe, then write their own rules from the ledger instead of being told them.
  • Concept. Energy and matter: total mass number and charge are conserved in every step.
  • Energy is qualitative. The log bar shows scale only. No energy calculations are needed.
  • Limits of the model. Nucleons are drawn as simple balls and are not to scale. Steps do not run in real time, since half-lives range from billions of years to under a microsecond.

Half-lives and decay energies are rounded values from standard nuclear data tables. Gamma rays are shown for the decays where they are especially common.
